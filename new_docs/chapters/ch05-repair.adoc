== Repair: The Vending Machine That Keeps Getting Up

*Time: about 25 minutes.*
*New ideas: repair distributions, availability versus reliability,
MTBDE, MDT.*

=== You will learn

* How to give a block a repair rule.
* Why *availability*, not reliability, is the right measure when things
  get fixed.
* What MTBDE and MDT tell you about your maintenance game.

=== You need

* <<ch-first-model>> through <<ch-distributions>> finished.
* The file `models/Vendo1.rbds`.
+
opsmodel:Vendo1[]

=== The story

A soda vending machine stands on a school corridor. It has two parts
that matter:

* the **Compressor** — fails about every 400 hours, but the repair
  technician fixes it in about 4 hours,
* the **Keypad** — fails about every 600 hours, and the replacement
  part ships slowly: a fixed 12 hours of waiting.

The machine serves for a full school year: **8760 hours**. It *will*
break many times — that is fine. The question is not "will it fail?"
but *"how much of the year is it actually selling soda?"*

=== One new idea: the repair rule

Until now, every block had Repair = None: die once, stay dead. Real
machines get repaired. Give a block a **repair distribution** and the
engine sends it to a repair queue when it fails:

. Block fails. The event log writes `Failed`, then `Repairing`.
. The engine rolls the repair dice for the fix duration.
. The block returns to service and starts a fresh failure clock.

Open `models/Vendo1.rbds` and its **Data Tables** panel (<<ch-first-model>>'s
spreadsheet-like view of every block, one row each):

image::vendo-data-tables.png[Data Tables dock: Vendo1's blocks, fail and repair distributions in one grid]

The same numbers, read off that grid:

[cols="3,2,2,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Block |Fail dist |F P1 |Repair |R P1 |R P2
|Compressor |Exponential |400 |Lognormal |4 |1.5
|Keypad |Exponential |600 |Fixed |12 |—
|===

Mission: stop at **8760 hours**, 20 trials.

=== Run it and meet the numbers

Press F5. The Results *Overview* tab now shows the repair-era metrics
(image from a 20-trial run):

image::vendo-results.png[Vendo1 results: availability, MTBDE, MDT]

[cols="3,5",options="header"]
|===
|Metric |Meaning
|Availability |fraction of the year the machine sells soda (here about 0.97)
|MTBDE |Mean Time Between Downs — about 237 hours between full stoppages
|MDT |Mean Down Time — about 7 hours per stoppage
|System Failures |about 35 stoppages per year
|===

Sanity check: 35 stoppages × 7 hours ≈ 245 down hours ≈ 2.8% of the
year. Availability ≈ 97.2%. The numbers agree with each other — a habit
worth copying.

=== Reliability vs availability: the exam question

Scroll to the *Final Results* tab. **Reliability is 0.00.** Correct!
The chance of surviving a whole year without a single stoppage is
essentially zero.

This is the most common beginner mistake in reliability work:

[cols="2,4,4",options="header"]
|===
| |Reliability |Availability
|Question |"Did it run non-stop to the end?" |"What fraction of time did it work?"
|Right when |failure is one-shot (spacecraft, fire alarm) |repair exists (cars, servers, vending machines)
|Our vending machine |0.00 |0.97
|===

A machine with reliability 0.00 and availability 0.97 is a *good*
machine. A fire alarm with those numbers is a criminal offense. Pick
the measure that matches the mission.

[TIP]
====
Two levers raise availability: fail *less often* (better parts,
redundancy — <<ch-redundancy>>, <<ch-voting>>) or stay down *less long* (faster repairs,
spares on the shelf). MDT tells you which lever is yours to pull. Here
the 12-hour keypad wait is the biggest single contributor.
====

=== One more idea: five letters that get mixed up constantly

MTBDE and MDT are *system*-level numbers — this report's own words for
"how the whole vending machine behaves". Reliability conversation also
uses three *component*-level letters, and confusing the two levels is
the most common mix-up in the field:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Term |Meaning

|MTTF
|Mean Time To Failure — one part's own average life (the Compressor's
Exponential mean, 400 h, *is* its MTTF)

|MTTR
|Mean Time To Repair — one part's own average fix time (the Keypad's
Fixed 12 h *is* its MTTR)

|MTBF
|Mean Time Between Failures — MTTF + MTTR for one repaired part, so
it only equals MTTF when repair is instant
|===

The system's MTBDE is *not* the smallest component's MTBF, and it is
not their sum either — it depends on the whole diagram (series parts
each contribute a chance to bring the system down; redundant parts
mostly don't). Reach for the exact formulas in the
xref:../docs/formulas.html[Formulas quick reference] rather than
guessing from a part's datasheet number.

.Limits of this model
[NOTE]
====
This model assumes a repair technician (or a replacement keypad) is
available the instant a part fails — there is no queue, no waiting for
a busy crew, no competition with other machines in the corridor for
the same spare part. <<ch-sparing>> starts to relax that assumption by
giving a part its own private stock of spares; a shared repair crew or
a shared spares warehouse across many machines needs the Resource
pooling this book only mentions in passing.
====

=== Check yourself

[cols="1,4"]
|===
|1 |Availability is 0.97 for a 8760-hour year. Roughly how many hours
was the machine down?
|2 |Why is reliability the wrong headline number here?
|3 |Which repair in this model is deterministic, and how do you know
from the table?
|===

.Answers
[%collapsible]
====
. About 260 hours (3% of 8760).
. Because the machine is repaired and will surely fail sometime in a
   year; the useful question is what fraction of time it works.
. The Keypad: its repair is *Fixed*, one number, 12 hours, no spread.
====

=== Exercise

Buy a spare keypad so repairs take 2 hours instead of 12: change the
Keypad's Repair Param 1 from `12` to `2`. Predict the new availability
*before* running. Run 100 trials. Was the spare shelf worth it?

.Answer
[%collapsible]
====
Availability rises to roughly 0.985–0.99. The keypad's share
of downtime shrinks by a factor of 6, so MDT drops from about 7 hours
to about 4 and the machine sells soda nearly all year.
====

=== What you learned

* A repair distribution turns a one-death story into a break–fix loop.
* Availability measures time-working fraction; reliability measures
  no-break-at-all odds.
* MTBDE and MDT split the problem into "fails too often" versus
  "stays down too long".
