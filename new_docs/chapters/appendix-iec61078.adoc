== Appendix: Reliability block diagrams and IEC 61078

IEC 61078 is the international standard for reliability block diagrams
(RBDs). It describes the logic of series, parallel and m-out-of-n
structures, and two families of evaluation:

* **qualitative** — minimal tie sets (success paths) and minimal cut sets
  (failure paths);
* **quantitative** — Boolean methods, binary decision diagrams and
  analytical availability/reliability formulae.

RAPTOR Studio uses the same diagram vocabulary but evaluates a model by
**Monte Carlo simulation**. That is a deliberate difference: simulation
handles repair, spares, phases, dependencies and limited resources that
the standard's simple closed-form formulae do not.

=== What RAPTOR supports

* Series, parallel and mixed structures.
* m-out-of-n voting, with identical or different blocks.
* Repaired and non-repaired blocks.
* Hierarchies as sub-RBDs with one In and one Out marker.
* Hot/cold standby, dependencies, spares, repair resources, PM and phases
  by simulation.

=== What RAPTOR does not do

RAPTOR does not calculate minimal cut/tie sets, Boolean/BDD expressions,
analytical importance factors, PFD~avg~ or the standard's analytical failure
intensities. (It does _estimate_ the Annex D importance measures by
re-simulation; see below.) It does not represent repeated/common blocks, multi-port
transfer gates, non-coherent (inverted) blocks, PAND/SEQ gates or a
common-cause beta factor.

Those are possible future work, not application features.

=== Reading the numbers

The standard's measures line up with RAPTOR's reports where the engine
actually computes them:

[cols="2,2,6",options="header"]
|===
|RAPTOR result |IEC 61078 |What it means

|Availability |10.3.1 / 10.3.2 |Simulated time-average system availability.
|Reliability |10.3.3 |Runs with no system failure over the horizon.
|System failures |10.3.4 (input) |Count of downing events; not a failure intensity.
|MTBDE, MDT, MTBM |Related dependability terms |Mean times from the event log; not Clause 10 outputs.
|===

Importance measures (Annex D) can be estimated for a run: switch on
**Run ▸ Analysis ▸ Importance measures**, then open **Results dock ▸
Final Results ▸ Importance**. It reports Birnbaum, RAW, RRW and
Vesely–Fussell importance for each block, with the sampling error of each,
and ranks the blocks by Vesely–Fussell (Birnbaum cannot separate the blocks
of a series system).
It costs one extra batch per block -- two with Birnbaum -- so it is off
unless you ask for it. Definitions and equations are on the Formulas page
(Help ▸ Formulas); the tab does not restate them.

This appendix is the in-app clause summary. The full clause-by-clause
table is `docs/IEC61078.md` in the repository.

''''

The practical rule: use the standard for analytical checks and
certification paperwork; use this simulator when the system's behaviour
depends on repair logistics, phases or interactions that the standard
says need simulation or a Markov/Petri model.
